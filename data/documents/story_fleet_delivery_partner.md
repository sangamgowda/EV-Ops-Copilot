# Fleet Story — Last-mile delivery partner moves 120 riders to electric

## Summary

A last-mile delivery operator in Bengaluru replaced its petrol
scooters with 120 electric units over four months. Cost per delivery
fell, rider turnover dropped, and the operator has signed for a
second city.

## Why they switched

- Fuel was their largest variable cost after rider pay.
- Petrol scooters needed an engine service every 3,000 km, taking a
  rider off the road for a day each time.
- Customers in two large residential areas had started asking for
  quieter, lower-emission deliveries.

## The rollout

The first 20 units went to the busiest hub as a six-week trial. The
remaining 100 followed in three batches once the trial numbers held.
Riders on the heaviest routes got the 5 kWh flagship; shorter routes
used the commuter model.

## Results after four months

| Measure | Before (petrol) | After (electric) |
|---|---|---|
| Energy or fuel cost per delivery | ₹4.10 | ₹1.35 |
| Scheduled service days per rider per quarter | 3 | 1 |
| Rider turnover per quarter | 18% | 11% |
| Deliveries per rider per day | 31 | 32 |

## What made it work

- A charging point at each hub, sized so every rider could top up
  during the lunch break.
- Loading guidance in rider training: keep total load within the
  rated payload. Riders who carried more saw noticeably shorter range.
- A single service contact for the whole fleet instead of per-rider
  workshop visits.

## Next

The operator has signed for 80 units in Hyderabad, on the same fleet
pricing terms.
